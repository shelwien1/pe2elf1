Thus Spoke the Hero Back from Another World
===========================================

0-01 "Prologue / or, the Epilogue of a Story Already Ended" — Part 1
-------------------

Looking at the midterm results handed back to him, he let out a quiet breath of relief.

His friend in the seat in front must have caught it, because he made a small "oh" and turned around with a smile.

"Sounds like you did pretty well this time."

"Yeah."

He answered with a smile of his own.

"I'm still nowhere near the top of the class, but compared to a little while ago my grades have recovered a lot. It's thanks to you. Thanks."

At the frank gratitude, his friend waved a hand as if embarrassed.

"Don't mention it. Seriously, you're embarrassing me."

"Am I? Well — they're certainly not good enough to get complacent about... but just clawing my way back this far was hard. I'm going to thank you for studying with me."

"Eh, it goes both ways. If I'm keeping you company while you're working seriously, then I don't slack off either."

The closing homeroom was over, and their classmates were drifting off in twos and threes, heading home or heading for club activities. His friend watched them go, speaking offhandedly.

He, too, was packing his things into his school bag as he looked down at the schoolyard through the window.

Below, the more eager members of the sports clubs had already started their warm-ups.

Off to one side were students walking out of the building in little groups — going off to have fun somewhere, most likely.

A thoroughly peaceful scene, the everyday landscape of a high-school student in modern Japan.

He watched that unremarkable sight through narrowed eyes.

"...What's up? You're spacing out."

His friend tilted his head and asked.

Nothing, he said, shaking his head at once, and changed the subject.

"Never mind that. About thanking you for the studying—"

"No, like I said, you don't have to. ...Ah, well, in that case—"

His friend glanced out the door toward the corridor.

Then he brought his eyes back and said, laughing teasingly:

"Introduce me to an underclassman girl sometime. Turns out I'm into younger girls."

"Turns out, huh... no, don't ask me for something like that. You know why, right?"

"I'm not asking *you*. Look, your escort's here. Makes a guy jealous, honestly."

"Mm—"

Sure enough, at the far end of the corridor, near the classroom door, a girl was waving at him.

"Ah, you finally looked my way. Let's head home, Daiki-senpai."

"...Right."

At his quiet answer, his friend — watching Daiki — shrugged.

"You two really do get along, though."

"...Does it look that way?"

"I mean, obviously. I don't know any other brother and sister that close. What's the deal — is she secretly a stepsister you're not actually related to?"

He fell silent, his expression complicated, and his friend laughed lightly and stretched.

"I'm kidding. Too much manga, that's all. Right, guess I'd better get to club..."

"Huh, is today a manga club day?"

"Nope, but I've got something to take care of. Meeting about my next piece."

"You've got an idea?"

"Who knows... Maybe I'll try an other-world story for once. Summoned to another world, becomes a hero, kills the Demon Lord, that sort of thing. Swinging a cool holy sword around."

"...Ha ha. Then I'll head home too. See you."

That said, he shoved the report card with «Daiki Kurosu» written on it into his bag and stood.

Out in the corridor, the girl from before fell quietly into step beside him, close enough to be leaning in.

"How were your midterm results?"

"Mm, well... finally about average, I'd say."

"That's wonderful. All that studying paid off, Daiki-senpai."

"Uh. Thanks, I guess."

"...Aren't you going to ask how Nagi did?"

The girl peered up at him. Daiki thought a moment before answering.

"No, I figured there was nothing to worry about where you're concerned. Your grades are always near the top, aren't they?"

"Hmm. Well then, let's say that's what it was. I'll let it pass, for senpai's sake."

The girl — Nagi Kurosu — smiled faintly.

After thinking about it a little, Daiki asked her:

"...I've been wondering for a while. Why do you call me «senpai»?"

"Is it strange?"

"When you ask it like that, I'm stuck for an answer. But at home it's «nii-san», isn't it?"

"Hee hee. Well, what's the harm — at school, at least? A little variety ought to keep senpai from getting bored of it. Call it a younger sister's consideration."

"...I don't think anyone gets bored of what they're called."

Well, if she was happy with it, maybe that was fine. Daiki left it at that.

Along the corridor, down the stairs, and out to the entrance hall.

He had nothing in particular planned afterward.

Reviewing the returned midterms wasn't urgent.

Nagi probably intended to walk home with him just like this. That was fine too, he thought — and then, right there at the entrance hall, he happened to spot a familiar figure taking her shoes out of the lockers.

"Ah..."

"—!"

She seemed to have noticed him as well.

Their eyes met for an instant, and hers were immediately averted. The girl who was supposed to have been his childhood friend hurried out of the building, all but fleeing from him.

"She's as rude as ever."

The words carried a barb, which was unusual for the soft-mannered Nagi.

Then again, after a display like that, perhaps it wasn't unreasonable.

Daiki shook his head lightly and said,

"Sorry, Nagi. There's somewhere I want to stop by, so would you go on ahead?"

"...Daiki-senpai."

"It's not like I'm going after her. She can't stand me; I'm not about to go out of my way to get close. I just want to detour somewhere, clear my head, and get the midterm review out of the way while I'm at it."

"In that case—"

"And I thought I'd stop at a bookshop too. So — that sort of thing."

"...Understood. Then I'll be going on ahead — **nii-san**."

Being called «nii-san» in that stiff tone was presumably some kind of statement on her part.

He might have done something unkind. But he genuinely did want a change of air, and — though he certainly couldn't say it out loud — having Nagi there unsettled him.

Even if he had to force it, he wanted to be alone.

"...A little sister, huh..."

Seeing Nagi off and then leaving the school building himself, Daiki murmured it under his breath.

He couldn't gauge the distance. Not with Nagi, his sister, and not with the childhood friend he was supposed to have been close to.

No — it wasn't only them.

It was his friend in the manga club, with whom he talked perfectly normally, and everything else besides.

—He understood the cause.

It was nothing other than the fact that he did not understand where he himself was standing.

Without even a clear sense of his own position, there was no way he could communicate properly with anyone other than himself.

"Where... am I, I wonder."

The murmur — in a way a thoroughly adolescent one, entirely appropriate to his age — dissolved into the clear pre-summer air.

Daiki narrowed his eyes against a refreshingly clear sky with few clouds, and sunlight at a pleasant temperature.

And yet.

Those words were a question he truly felt, from the heart.

Yes — a **question**.

Not a worry, not an inner conflict: a pure riddle, the kind that ought to have an answer.

Where is this place?

Where am I?

For about a year now, Daiki had been thinking about that without pause.

This world was nothing but wrongness, everywhere he looked.

"—**The thing is**, **I never had a little sister named Nagi**..."

　　　　　※

In the end he'd stayed until the place closed.

Pushed out by a staff member's "We'll be closing shortly," Daiki Kurosu was walking through town at ten in the evening — an hour that could fairly be called night.

"I concentrated too hard... What do I do? It'd be awkward to get picked up for curfew."

Socially speaking, Daiki's station was that of a second-year high-school student.

In uniform, needless to say. Strolling brazenly through the entertainment district was hardly advisable.

After a moment's thought, Daiki decided to take the back streets home. It would be a bit of a detour, but he didn't mind moving his body. He'd get off the main roads and make his way home that way.

Carrying his school bag, and a plastic bag with the two paperbacks he'd bought at the bookshop he'd visited before the café.

From any angle, he'd look like an ordinary male high-school student on his way home from school.

That was what everyday life was. An utterly commonplace, peaceful existence, the kind found anywhere.

Was such a thing sturdy? Or was it fragile?

Daiki, at least, was not in possession of the answer.

But even so.

"Ngh—?! What, is this... I feel... sick—?!"

—He understood immediately that he had walked straight into the moment when everyday life (*it*) was destroyed.

What struck Daiki then, out of nowhere, was an overwhelming revulsion.

It wasn't that anything was wrong with his body. There was no pain. Only a violent feeling of disgust, as though it had been poured directly into his brain — a sensation of being sick to his stomach with no way around it.

And with it an agitation that would not be satisfied until he cut the sensation off at its source, right now.

"...Tch."

And so Daiki's steps drifted, unsteadily, in a certain direction, without his even being aware of it.

Off the road home, toward somewhere emptier of people — no, toward **a space that no human should have been able to enter**.

That the place had been made **that way** was likewise beyond Daiki's awareness.

The feeling was close to drunkenness. As if strong spirits saturated the air and he had breathed them in. He turned into a narrow alley, and by degrees the surroundings opened out into a spot hemmed in by run-down buildings.

"...Huh. Why am I somewhere like this...?"

It was a place he would never have passed through on his way home. Not so much a detour as the wrong direction entirely.

He understood that this was **wrong**, and still his feet would not stop carrying him forward.

And so what came next was the natural conclusion.

Daiki turned the corner of the alley ahead of him.

Past the bend was a slightly wider dead end, with moonlight falling in from above.

But what stole his eyes more than anything was the too-vivid red that coloured the place.

The whole surface was stained with red in great quantity, as though a can of paint had been overturned, and it ran everywhere across the ground in geometric figures, following some fixed set of rules.

More abnormal still was that the red patterns drawn on the ground were giving off a faint light. The reason he could make them out clearly even in the gloom was the red glow welling up from underfoot rather than the moonlight.

The smell of iron stung his nose.

Daiki understood perfectly well that it was **a magic circle drawn in blood**.

"...This is bad, isn't it."

The instant he murmured it, the faint light being cast drained away rapidly, and went to zero.

Like this, the atmosphere was exactly that of a grisly murder scene. And in fact, if this much blood had come from a single person, there was no way that person was still alive.

Fortunately, though — if that was the right word — he could find nothing resembling a corpse in the vicinity.

All that was here now was a magic circle drawn in blood. That was all.

"What am I... supposed to do about this? Should I call the police or something...?"

Or would it be wiser to pretend he hadn't seen it and turn on his heel?

The briefest hesitation.

What proved fatal, though, was not that instant, but the fact of having come to this place at all.

If it was a matter of being too late, then by this point there was nothing Daiki could have done.

An instant later, Daiki sprang lightly, the smallest of steps, forward.

Twisting his body, turning as he leapt.

Something was being swung through the spot he had occupied a heartbeat earlier — a fact he confirmed by sensation rather than by sight.

—**He had been attacked**.

Two people were surprised there.

If the two were measured against each other, the greater surprise surely belonged not to Daiki, who had realised he was being attacked from behind, but to the assailant whose surprise knifehand had been dodged so easily.

"That was close..."

Daiki murmured it quietly.

It probably wasn't a reply, but the other person present spoke, with a note of understanding.

"Dodged it, huh... Well, of course you would, standing in a place like this. I was hedging my bets, and I suppose that's what you'd call softness on my part. Right — I'll chalk it up to tuition."

He heard a tap, tap — the sound of a toe striking the ground.

Keeping his distance, narrowing his eyes, Daiki caught the source of the sound and the words at the far end of his gaze.

—It was a girl.

About his own age, perhaps. Dressed all in black — some school's uniform? — she wore it and melted into the darkness.

Her short hair was black as well; the only feature that stood out in the night was her crimson eyes.

They lowered slightly, and with a sigh the girl went on, sounding as if it were a chore.

"I don't want to drag this out, so I'll say only what needs saying: if you're going to surrender, do it now. I'm here as the deputy of this city's administrator, more or less. You know what that means. I'm no champion of justice either — do it somewhere else and I won't come after you — but if you're going to pull something in this place, I can't afford to be lenient."

"..."

Vaguely, at least.

—Wait, am I being taken for the culprit who painted this place in blood?

That was what Daiki gathered.

He'd been sure the one who attacked him was the culprit and that he was about to be silenced; but if that was the situation, he might be able to clear up the misunderstanding.

Though even so, the fact that a girl like this was chasing the culprit struck him as strange.

"Um. I think there might be some kind of misunderstanding here..."

At any rate, Daiki opened his mouth to talk it out.

To her, however, the remark apparently rang thoroughly false.

The girl's crimson eyes pierced him sharply.

"—Are you making fun of me?"

"Huh? No, I didn't mean—"

"No, it's fine. I'm used to it, so that part's fine. But I'd rather not be taken for someone you can fob off with words like that. Fine. Understood. —I've delivered my warning properly, at least."

"Wh-whoa... I get the feeling this conversation is rolling in a bad direction."

At the thoroughly unwelcome premonition, the corner of Daiki's mouth twitched.

The girl, meanwhile, sank slightly and took a guard with no opening in it. Plainly a battle stance.

—Oh, for heaven's sake. No help for it.

Daiki steeled himself.

When sparks come falling on you, you have to run at full speed or you'll get burned.

Honestly, he had almost no grasp of what was going on, but the one future that was perfectly clear was that at this rate he was going to be attacked.

Her standing on the side with the way out was a nuisance, but he was confident he could manage something.

And then—

"—Hah!"

The girl burst forward.

0-02 "Prologue / or, the Epilogue of a Story Already Ended" — Part 2
-------------------

Kicking hard off the ground, she came in low and drove straight inside his guard in one motion.

Sinking from darkness into darkness, the movement was hard to follow.

—Not going to react in time—!

All Daiki could do was cross his arms and block the blow.

Fortunately, comparing builds, even Daiki — who was hardly a big man — far outmatched the girl.

The guard should hold.

That such optimism did not apply was a lesson Daiki was made to learn with his own body.

"Rrraaah!"

"Gkh—"

From a posture that was practically a crouch, the fist came up along an impossible, springing trajectory.

The girl's right fist buried itself into the middle of his crossed left arm, and Daiki felt a crunch of breaking bone from inside his own body — the blow was strong enough to make him think so, at least.

"Ghk, gh, gaah—?!"

His body was flung back and he rolled across the concrete. It left scrapes here and there, but the impact was such that he couldn't spare attention for anything so minor.

The force was impossible. For a moment he genuinely thought he'd been shot through from the ground by a pile driver (*pile bunker*).

Daiki somehow got his posture back and pushed himself up onto one knee, but the absurd strength of the pain from his left arm wouldn't permit him anything more.

"Ow, ow... ugh, damn it, seriously...!"

"...Hmm?"

The girl, meanwhile, made a sound like she was questioning something.

He wanted to say that *he* was the one who ought to be tilting his head, but of course he couldn't.

"You jumped back and killed the force...? No, but— actually, before that—"

—What is this person talking about?

Daiki genuinely wondered. He'd rather she didn't say it so casually.

Granted, he'd tried to do something like that.

Even so, it hadn't gone as neatly as in a manga or an anime; his left arm was very likely just plain broken, and rolling across the ground had hurt too.

It was more that he'd merely avoided having his organs rupture and his whole body burst from a single hit.

The girl, though, apparently wasn't questioning things for that reason alone.

"Actually— huh, wait. That uniform... huh, you're a student?"

At this point the girl widened her eyes as if only now recognising what Daiki looked like.

"...Is she seriously telling me she hadn't noticed until this very second...?"

It had been dark, true. But even so.

Wrapped in a sense of dissatisfaction, plus the searing pain in his left arm, Daiki watched the situation unfold.

And then, right in front of him, the girl said:

"—Um. Who are you?"

"Am I allowed to take that as an admission that you decked someone by mistake?"

The girl pressed her lips into a flat line and made an entertaining face, breaking out in a cold sweat.

"No. No, no, no, there's no way. There's no way an ordinary person is in a place like this. I checked properly at the start, too. —Ah, th-that's it! The way you moved is impossible!"

"..."

"Nngh, no, but the age really is too far off, and the report said he wasn't this much of a fighter, which is exactly why the job came to me in the first place... no, but, huh...? Then who are you?!"

For just an instant, he considered asking who *she* was in return.

He gave up on that and answered honestly instead. His arm hurt; he wasn't in the mood for games.

"...Daiki Kurosu. High-school student."

Having said that much, he considered whether something more was called for.

And Daiki appended these words to his self-introduction:

"—A perfectly ordinary one."

"Like hell a perfectly ordinary high-school student moves like that!"

The girl clutched her head and howled it.

...Well, fair enough.

Daiki thought so too. Even if he'd had no other options, he had gone and done something an average high-school student couldn't. Which seemed to have got him strangely suspected.

Still, if one spoke strictly of the present, there wasn't a single lie in what he'd just said.

"...For now, is it fine to say the misunderstanding's cleared up? I'd appreciate an explanation of why I got jumped out of nowhere, if possible."

Daiki stood as he asked. But the girl still seemed to be in a state of confusion.

"Huh, wait. No, seriously, wait. I still can't accept this!"

"...Which part?"

"Which part... Okay, I accept that you're not the target I'm chasing. But being told you're a perfectly ordinary high-school student — that part I can't accept."

Settling her confusion, the girl spoke.

The misunderstanding aside, her wariness had not yet been lowered.

"What are you? I'd like a detailed explanation from your side too."

"...Even if you say that."

"I know I'm being unreasonable. If I find out you're unrelated, I'll take whatever responsibility afterward. But as long as I'm here on a job, I can't just leave a suspicious character alone."

"Ah... no, that's not what I meant."

Explaining his circumstances was difficult.

Well — saying it was easy enough.

But he couldn't imagine, not by any stretch, that he'd be believed.

What to do, Daiki wondered.

He'd demanded an explanation a moment ago, but it was all starting to feel like a hassle, and his arm hurt, and honestly he'd rather just be let go. Or be looked after, that would do too.

The girl watched him waver, let out a breath, and then said:

"All right. I'm going to contact an acquaintance for a second, and then—"

It happened in that instant.

—All at once, the magic circle of blood began to glow again.

"Magical energy?! Why— it *is* you after all!"

"It's not, it wasn't me! And what did you just say? Magical energy?!"

The red light gathered strength.

Unlike before, it brightened in an instant — and in an instant went out again.

Neither Daiki nor, it seemed, even the girl understood everything about what was happening and why.

With an expression of complete incomprehension, the girl threw a glance at Daiki.

"It reacted...? To me? Or else... Hey, you—"

"—Look out!"

"Eek?!"

It was reflexive. This time Daiki was the one shouting, and he dived at the girl.

Caught off guard, the girl was borne down onto the ground under him.

"What are you—"

"Don't struggle, my arm hurts! And get up, now!"

With the situation changing by the second, the confusion was mutual.

Even so, Daiki was the slightly calmer of the two.

He got off the girl before he could be misunderstood again, and turned his eyes to the front.

The girl saw it as well.

Standing at the end of his gaze: a black, four-legged creature with an enormous body.

"Wha..."

No — whether it was accurate to call that thing a creature, the girl at least could not say with confidence.

That black bulk was far too inorganic to be called a beast, and nonetheless it held murderous intent.

"...Ngh..."

Groaning, Daiki pressed a hand to his arm. He'd overexerted it and made it worse.

Because he'd shielded her — and once she understood that, what the girl would do was decided.

"...Thanks. Stay back, I'll handle it."

She was done thinking about what he really was.

In this situation, he had protected her, a total stranger.

That debt had to be repaid.

"Much obliged... though it feels like a weird thing to be saying."

"Well, I'm the one who broke your arm... I said I was sorry."

"Never mind that — can you beat it? That thing."

"Who knows? I've never seen anything like it... a familiar or something of that sort, I'd assume. Though for that, its presence feels off somehow."

"...No," Daiki said.

"**That's a monster**."

"...What are you talking about?"

"Hard to explain. Anyway, just think of it as the type of creature that pours its heart and soul into killing humans. That's roughly correct."

"What kind of worst-possible creature is that. ...So, does it have a weakness?"

The girl decided not to think about the details.

There were countless questions.

Naturally so, when a mere high-school student who called himself perfectly ordinary was speaking about something even she had never seen as though it were familiar.

But it was fine.

Because for now, for this moment alone, the girl had decided to trust him.

"It has a core. Around the crown of its head, probably."

"A core..."

"You can think of it as something like a stone. Crush that and it goes down."

"I see... yes, if I focus and look, I can make it out."

"I can't see it myself, but... well, if you can, that's fine. Conversely, attacking anywhere else is almost meaningless, so keep that in mind. ...Can you do it?"

"Easily."

As she spoke, the girl turned side-on to the black beast. She extended an arm in front of her, palm up, index finger pointed — the posture of someone taking aim with a ranged weapon, if one had to compare it to something.

Conversely, it was a stance that would be hard to recover from if the thing pounced.

Daiki couldn't read her intent.

Even so — Daiki said nothing.

And then.

"—«Thorn»—"

Just those words, and nothing else.

With the word as a trigger, something black was loosed from the girl's fingertip — or so it looked to Daiki.

Strictly speaking he hadn't been able to see it. It was dark here, and above all the movement had been too fast.

But as a result, whatever the girl had loosed from her fingertip had unmistakably pierced the monster through the crown of its head. Its core crushed, unable to maintain its existence, the monster dispersed into the air like mist.

"...What was that just now..."

Daiki's words were less a question than a stunned murmur; the girl shrugged and answered lightly.

"Just what it looked like. My magecraft."

"...Your what?"

"Is that so surprising? You're throwing around words like 'monster,' so it can't be your first time seeing a magus. And come to that, aren't you a magus too?"

"No, well... not really, on both counts."

"...What is that supposed to mean. I really don't understand this person at all... Ahem!"

The girl cleared her throat with pointed deliberateness, then slowly approached Daiki.

Standing in front of him, she extended a hand toward him. And at the same time she said:

"Let's start over. I'm Narumi. —Narumi Oki. A magus of no importance. And you're, um... Daiki, was it?"

"Mm, yeah. Daiki Kurosu."

"Then — nice to meet you. Not that it'll be a long acquaintance."

"...Likewise, I guess?"

Thinking that this had turned into something strange, Daiki answered the handshake.

The girl — Oki — smiled thinly as she took his hand, and pulled it hard toward her.

"Whoa—"

Her face closed in all at once.

Seen properly, Oki was a girl with well-arranged features.

"So? I'd be glad if Daiki's side would reveal his true identity around now too."

Crimson eyes fixed straight through him. Pressed by them, Daiki spoke.

"Ah, no... it's not that I have a true identity or anything; the perfectly ordinary high-school student part isn't a lie. It's just—"

"Just?"

"—**The thing is**, **a while back**, **I got sent to another world**."

"......Huh?"

"I got a holy sword there, killed the Demon Lord... well, travelled for about five years. And then last year I came back to Earth, sort of? That's about the shape of it, anyway..."

"..."

"No, um... er. Narumi-san?"

"Oki is fine, Daiki. Hm?"

"Is it. No, um, that's not the issue. Oki. Oki-san, the... my hand hurts."

Oki was showing him a sweet, lovely smile, right in front of his face.

So she could make an expression like that too, Daiki found himself vaguely impressed — except this was no time for that.

"Um. Ow. The handshake hurts, it really hurts, you're squeezing way too hard, ow. Seriously strong, strong, strong, ow."

"Hey, Daiki. That just now — would that be you making fun of me, I wonder?"

"That's really not my intention. No, I'm honestly being completely serious, I'm sorry."

"Then did you hit your head or something?"

"My left arm got punched through, but— I mean ow, ow, it really hurts, you're going to crush my right hand too!"

"—There's no way I can believe that!"

"That's exactly why I didn't want to say it... And magi are about the same thing, aren't they? There's no way people like that exist as a matter of course — think about it with common sense."

"C-common sense?! I'm being lectured on common sense by a guy who just announced he went to another world?!"

—But, unfortunately.

Just as, for instance, **Narumi Oki was in fact a magus**,

**what Daiki Kurosu said was fact**, **and he had once truly been the hero who saved another world**.

He had carried the holy sword, been called a Hero, and struck down the Demon Lord with overwhelming power.

And then, returning to Earth, he lost every scrap of that power and went back to being nothing more than **a perfectly ordinary high-school student who was just a bit used to the last-ditch moment**.

And therefore, he does not know.

The fact that this Earth conceals an extraordinary to rival even another world.

That magi, or the Empowered, monsters, inhuman things, supernatural existences out of science fiction and fantasy, genuinely do exist — Daiki Kurosu has no way of knowing it.

So.

Even if the everyday life he clawed back should be threatened by such supernatural beings.

He has no strength left with which to fight.

Nor can he acquire any.

There is no one who knows that he was a hero, no way to prove it, and the boy is not permitted to resist what is unreasonable.

And in fact, he has been dragged in.

That he is unhelpfully calm, that he can, after a fashion, cope with the situation — these facts are nothing but a curse-like after-effect luring him toward an extraordinary he does not want.

Though he truly is an ordinary person with no power whatsoever, he ends up suspected of being an extraordinary man harbouring some secret.

But. Even so.

—**Daiki Kurosu is nothing more than a perfectly ordinary high-school student**.

1-01 "The Former Hero, and the Witch" — 1
-------------------

If asked whether there had been any warning signs, Daiki would have to answer that there had been none.

He went to sleep, woke up, and before he knew it he was in another world.

It had been so abrupt that there was no other way to describe it: before he knew it Daiki had been transferred to an other world he had never seen or heard of, and there was no option but to live through it — put into words, that was all there was to it.

He had been a first-year high-school student at the time.

It had been a brutal road.

Battles in which it would not have been strange to die at any moment.

Even so, together with a holy sword that possessed a will and companions he could rely on, Daiki had adventured the length and breadth of the world — of the other world — and at last succeeded in his goal, the slaying of the Demon Lord.

And then, with the same abruptness as when he had arrived, he came to himself back here on Earth.

—And with no passage of time whatsoever.

　　　　　※

"Um. Hold on a second, let me get this straight."

—Afterward.

Saying she would "clear up," Oki had wiped away what remained on the spot — the thing that appeared to be a blood magic circle — until it was clean. And in almost an instant, at that.

Some sort of magic — or rather, «magecraft», as Oki called it — must have been employed.

Daiki was well used to the sight of magic itself from the other world, but seeing it on Earth was a first. The system itself seemed to be different; what had been done, Daiki could not begin to understand.

Once the disposal was finished, Oki went on to treat Daiki's arm.

Though as she put it: "It's strictly first aid, all right? I did use healing magecraft, technically, but frankly at my level of skill it's a drop of water on a hot stone. So this is about all I can do. ...Sorry."

Instead, she first touched his arm, and then with some mysterious pitch-black thread-like thing produced from that hand (Daiki had no idea whatsoever what it was) she wound his left arm around and around.

The black thread had no thickness to it — no mass, rather — that he could feel; the sensation was that of a shadow, if one had to compare.

"...It's gone pitch black."

Daiki looked at the thread wound directly against his skin as he said it.

1-05 "The Former Hero's Everyday Life" — 1

The night passed.

If one insisted on calling the return from the extraordinary to the ordinary a rehabilitation, then Daiki could be said to have spent a year at it, and his body was only now beginning to grow used to it.

Adapting to the extraordinary that was another world might, in hindsight, have been the easier task—

Looking back, Daiki thought so.

He couldn't claim with a straight face that his mental state had been stable when he was first thrown into the other world, but when it was a matter of life and death, one couldn't spend all day complaining.

The result was that he adapted to life in the other world by being cornered into it.

Even so.

"Hmmmm..."

He remembered none of it.

He couldn't keep up at all—

The school being an academically ambitious one, recovering the study knowledge lost during his long life in the other world could fairly be called the problem that tormented him most.

Even now he was only at the level of having maybe, possibly, clawed back to average.

—Thank God he'd been transferred in the spring of his first year.

Daiki was fairly seriously grateful for that. If it had been right before entrance exams it would have been unwatchable.

He didn't consider the gap large enough to be worth minding.

Even so, he was made to feel something like a discrepancy from time to time.

That he'd rather hide it, if possible, was also Daiki's undisguised truth.

"So, you've got something on your mind?"

His friend asked again. Apparently he was genuinely worried.

Had he looked that troubled? Tilting his head, Daiki answered with gratitude in his voice.

"Just a bit of wondering what to do from here."

"Ahh, career stuff, then."

"Mm, more or less. Broadly speaking, yes."

It wasn't accurate, but framing it that way was less trouble.

He could hardly consult anyone about having befriended a magus and being dragged into an incident.

Still less when what troubled him wasn't the incident itself.

"Even so, you're planning to go on to university, right?"

"Well, I intend to get into a university. I don't have anywhere in particular I'm aiming for."

That had been Daiki's thinking before he went to the other world.

Nothing very admirable: the easy, difficult daydream of vaguely aiming for a good university and vaguely wanting a job at a good company.

And what of his present self, by contrast?

Daiki didn't know.

The simple matter of just going back to how he'd been was, for some reason, beyond him.

Somewhere there was always a hard-to-articulate wrongness clinging to him.

—Is it really all right for me to stay like this?

Isn't there something else, something I want to do, something I want to aim for—?

Is it all right for me to be in this place — at all?

That sense of wrongness had, for a year now, refused to be wiped away.

"Well, I figure everyone's like that at this point... hey, Daiki."

His friend, who had been listening with narrowed eyes, glanced away toward the outside of the classroom.

"Something wrong?"

"No... isn't it kind of noisy?"

A school after classes is by definition a noisy place. There was nothing strange about it.

But now that he mentioned it, something in the air from the corridor side was certainly different from usual.

"Hm...?"

Daiki turned his eyes that way too.

Which was when he noticed: for some reason every student in the corridor had stopped walking and was looking in a single direction.

As though someone extremely conspicuous were walking toward them from that way.

"—Ah, here it is. Class 2-5."

And then a voice came flying into the classroom.

Along with that familiar voice, a familiar face popped in, peering into the room.

It felt as though his hearing and his sight had been punched — not by force, but by freshness.

"There you are. Morning, Daiki. I've come to pick you up!"

Her face brightening at once, the girl he'd met last night — the magus — gave Daiki a little wave.

The same black uniform as yesterday. A visitor's pass hung from her chest, pointlessly correctly.

"..................Oki... -san?"

"Yep. Come on, let's go. Up, up."

There, as though it were nothing at all, stood Narumi Oki.

Every gaze in the classroom, and in the corridor beyond it, stabbed blatantly into Daiki.

A considerable crack tore across his ordinary life — so Daiki would later recall it.

It was an «Assault».

"Why... are you here— ah, no, ahh."

He had all sorts of things he wanted to ask, and no time for any of them.

Daiki hurriedly gathered his things and stood. His friend, right beside him, grabbed his shoulder.

And, of course, asked this:

"...What's going on?"

"I'd like to know myself... Sorry, I don't want to explain it here."

"No, but, hey — that uniform, she's a middle-schooler, isn't she...?"

"How can you tell just from the uniform— ...no, wait, seriously?"

—Breaking news! Oki! The middle-schooler hypothesis!!

That was its own kind of confusing, and there was no time for it!

"Sorry, I'll give you the full explanation at a class reunion in about five years, so, later!"

"—That's way too much later!"

"It's fine, life goes by in a flash!"

Firing off a remark he didn't understand himself, Daiki fled into the corridor.

He tilted his head as he asked.

At the question Oki made a deeply troubled face.

"Mmm... that's hard to explain. She's not a comrade of mine, and she's definitely not someone in the same position. Well, her skills are reliable, so — a treating physician I called in, I suppose."

"I heard she isn't a doctor..."

"That's, right. If anything, calling her a scientist would be closer, I think."

"...Huh...?"

Oki's answer didn't get to the point, but it wasn't that she was hiding something; it genuinely was hard to explain.

For now, Daiki registered that she was unquestionably not a simple opponent.

"More importantly, you should get treated too."

Oki said.

"Why haven't you? Getting *you* treated was the whole reason I contacted Tsunashino-san in the first place."

"...Well, you were more badly hurt."

That was true, and since arriving here Tsunashino had immediately set about treating Oki.

There'd been no opportunity for Daiki to have his own injuries treated, and in any case he'd fallen asleep right afterward.

He'd thought recovering that way was ultimately best.

—He hadn't imagined Tsunashino would heal injuries this easily.

"Besides, even if you say get treated..."

As he muttered it, a voice came.

"—I wasn't given treatment fees for two."

Through the sliding door Oki had flung open a moment ago, the Kurumi Tsunashino in question appeared.

She stated it as though it were obvious, then narrowed her eyes at the sight of the two of them.

"Were you that sort of relationship? I'll take my leave if I'm in the way."

"Whuh?! Ah, n-no, that's not— what are you saying?!"

After all, Oki was in a posture that was nearly an embrace of Daiki on the futon.

She could well imagine how that looked from the outside. Her face went scarlet and she hurriedly separated.

"I wasn't aware I'd said anything strange enough to be scolded for — anyway."

Tsunashino, for her part, was matter-of-fact.

As though she had no interest, she changed the subject and asked them:

"Do you need treatment? If it isn't a serious injury I recommend an ordinary hospital. Insurance doesn't apply, so frankly it's expensive."

Some sort of back-alley-doctor arrangement, presumably, Daiki guessed loosely.

At any rate, an ordinary high-school student like him wasn't going to be able to pay high medical fees.

"I'm fine, reall— owww?!"

Oki lightly smacked the arm of Daiki as he began to decline.

"Why are you refusing?!"

"No, because I don't have that kind of money—"

"I'll handle that somehow! Just let Tsunashino-san look at you already!"

Given an order, he had no choice.

And if it meant his family needn't find out he was injured, it was convenient.

Maybe he should look for a part-time job — thinking that, Daiki stood.

"This way, then."

Saying it, Tsunashino withdrew immediately into the living room.

A brisk sort of person, he thought.

With that impression, Daiki followed after her. Oki walked along behind him.

"Sit, please. I'll examine you first."

Guided as instructed, Daiki sat on the sofa in the middle of the room.

The same place where he'd made the contract with Oki the night before last.

As he half-remembered it, Tsunashino touched his arm.

"A fracture. I see no other major injuries; any magical impairment?"

"Huh? Ah, no. I'm not a magus, so there shouldn't be anything like that."

"...I see. I'm afraid I don't understand magecraft. Well, if it's purely physical damage this will be quick. Hold out an arm. Either side is fine."

"Right..."

Wouldn't one normally hold out the injured one?

Thinking so, Daiki held out his left arm, and for some reason Tsunashino narrowed her eyes at him.

"...Hold still, then."

The instant she said it, she brought down something metallic she'd been holding in her right hand — like a stamp — and pressed it against Daiki's left hand.

"Ow, ouch?!"

There was almost no pain from the device itself, but it resonated through the bone and Daiki cried out.

Watching this with a face that said it was only to be expected, Tsunashino said a single line:

"You should have held out the right arm."

"I'd have appreciated being told that first..."

"I did tell you."

"No, that's true, but..."

Something about it didn't sit right.

At the sullen Daiki, Tsunashino released his hand and said one word.

"Done."

"Huh. Wait, that's it? Immobilisation or—"

"I can apply some if you want, but you'll just be taking it off this afternoon."

"Sorry?"

At the outrageous statement, Daiki's eyes widened.

But Tsunashino's expression still barely moved.

"If it's merely damage, it will heal shortly. Though do not forget there is a price for that. You say you're not a magus, but this isn't magecraft either. Don't mistake it for omnipotence."

"...What did you do?"

"**I injected medical nanomachines into your body.**"

"————"

Since what she was saying was far too baffling, Daiki found himself staring at her.

Tsunashino, thus stared at, didn't move her expression in the slightest.

"That's embarrassing."

"You're not embarrassed at all..."

He somehow felt Urei's speech patterns had rubbed off on him.

Not understanding, Daiki shifted his gaze to Oki.

She shook her shoulders with a wry smile.

"—Tsunashino-san is with the «Gakkai»."

Come to think of it, he felt he'd heard that word somewhere recently. That said,

"No, hold on, I don't follow. She's with an academic society, and that means what?"

"The word's probably different from the one you're imagining. What I said is short for «Gakujutsu Sōkai» — the Grand Academic Assembly."

"Gakujutsu Sōkai...?"

At the unfamiliar term Daiki tilted his head.

Oki laughed a little, raised a finger, and continued:

"There's an organisation by that name, but in this case it's quicker to take it as referring to one of the three worlds. Right — putting only the essentials very, very simply, what it means is—"

Oki's gaze turned toward Tsunashino.

Drawn along by it, Daiki shifted his gaze as well, and the words that reached his ears were:

"Tsunashino-san possesses **scientific technology from far, far in the future of the present day**."

And Kurumi Tsunashino — who, depending on how one heard it, was a far stranger existence than any magus —

let her gaze slide over the speechless Daiki, and as though it were nothing at all:

"Strictly speaking, it isn't I who possess it."

readily acknowledged that what Oki had said was essentially fact.
